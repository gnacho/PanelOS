PanelOS

One look, wherever PanelOS appears

PanelOS is open firmware for smart home wall panels. It replaces the closed software a panel came with by a small Linux system with a fast native Home Assistant dashboard, an assistant screen and a voice satellite. No cloud, nothing the panel did not need.

These guidelines describe one identity for everything that carries the name: the panel's own screens, the Home Assistant integration, the repository, the documentation and the website. Every file mentioned here is in the brand kit; every measure comes from the same geometry as those files.

The idea

A letter in light

The mark is the letter P in Morse code: dot, dash, dash, dot. Set in two rows it becomes a sign of its own, and it reads the same after a half turn, because the code is the same from either end.

P

Morse code is a signal that is only on or off, light or sound, and one of the oldest ways of sending text. PanelOS lives in that world: a screen in the wall that lights up when someone comes near, listens, and answers.

Look again and the mark is also a small dashboard: two small cards and two wide ones in two rows, the kind of screen PanelOS draws.

And it tells where PanelOS comes from. Many panels are left with software that was closed, forgotten or of little use. PanelOS gives them a second life, and on the screen the code lights up one element after the other, like embers catching.

The idea

Four principles

01
Signal, not noise

Four elements, two colours, plenty of dark around them. Nothing is added to the mark, and the screens around it stay as calm as it is.

02
Code you can read

The mark is not a picture of something: it is the letter itself, written in an open code anyone can look up. Open like the firmware.

03
Calm on the wall

PanelOS lives in homes, in kitchens and halls, at night too. Dark grounds, no glare, red only where something is lit.

04
A second life

Devices that were set aside get to work again. The identity is quiet and durable, made to last as long as the panels it runs on.

The mark

Built on one unit

Everything is measured in one unit, u: the height of a dash and the width of a dot.

  • A dot is a circle u wide. A dash is 3u long and u high, with fully round ends: the proportions of Morse code itself.
  • Elements in a row stand u apart; the two rows stand u apart. The mark is 5u wide and 3u high.
  • The top row is dot and dash, the bottom row dash and dot. Read across, top to bottom, that is the letter P: · − − ·.
  • The mark is symmetric about its centre (the grey point). Turned half a turn it is the same; it is never turned a quarter turn, mirrored or tilted.
  • Dots are always Signal red: they are the lit part of the code. Dashes take the text colour of the ground: Ink on light, Paper or white on dark.

The mark

Five forms, one look

Mark

Where the name is already present or space is small: favicons, the panel's status line, small print.

App icon

The mark on an Ink badge with continuous corners (a superellipse). Home Assistant's icon, avatars, launchers.

Logo

Mark and wordmark side by side: the first choice everywhere else.

Stacked logo

For square or narrow spaces: covers, the panel's boot screen.

Signature

The whole short name, P O S, in Morse on one line. A rule or a closing line, never instead of the logo.

The logo's wordmark is Space Grotesk Medium. Its cap height is 4u, so the mark is three quarters of it, and the mark stands 2u before the P with its centre on the middle of the cap height. Stacked, the mark is 1.6 times larger and centred 3.2u above the cap line. These proportions were chosen in use; they are not changed.

Four colour versions

Colour on light

Signal dots, Ink dashes. The first choice.

Signal dots, Paper dashes (white on the panel).

The mark

What not to do

The mark as it is: two Signal dots, two dashes in the ground's text colour, in this order, upright. Everything below changes one of these.
Swapped colours

The dots are always Signal: they are the lit part of the code.

Changed order

Dash and dot first would no longer read as P.

Turned

Never a quarter turn, a mirror image or a tilt. A half turn is the same mark.

Stretched

Never stretched or squashed: dots stay round, dashes 3 units long.

Outlined

Always solid. No outlines, no hollow versions.

Effects

No shadows, gradients or bevels. The glow belongs to the start-up only.

Other colours

Signal and Ink, Paper or white. No other colours, even for a theme.

Other typeface

The wordmark is the outlined Space Grotesk file, never retyped.

Mostly dark and light, a little red. Signal is the colour of a lit element: about one part in twenty.

The pattern

The code as texture

The pattern repeats the short name, P O S, in Morse, row after row.

  • Elements as in the mark: dot u, dash 3u, u between elements, 3u between letters, 3u between two repeats.
  • Rows are 2.6u apart, and each row starts 7u further to the left, so no column lines up.
  • It is always quiet: on dark grounds Ember dots and Ash dashes, on light grounds Blush dots and Stone dashes. Full colour, as in the sample here, only to explain the construction.
PanelOS brand guidelinesThe pattern
Version 1.0 · November 202617
The pattern

Where it goes

  • Yes: covers and dividers, the README header, the social preview, the website's first screen, the panel's start-up, the back of printed matter.
  • A calm area behind the logo: the pattern stops at least 2u around it, or a ground-coloured panel covers it.
  • Scale: the pattern's unit is at most a quarter of the mark's unit next to it, so the mark is always the largest piece of code on the page.
  • No: behind running text, tables or form fields; in Signal and Ink at full strength; animated in the background of a working screen.

Timing follows the code: one unit is 100 ms (12 words per minute), a dot lasts one unit, a dash three, with one unit between elements. Easing is a cubic ease-out; nothing bounces. The start-up is the only place where the mark moves or glows.

Motion and sound

The tone

A classic Morse tone: a clean sine at 700 Hz, keyed in the code's own rhythm.

100 ms300 ms300 ms100 ms
ToneSine, 700 Hz, the same pitch for dots and dashes
Speed12 words per minute: one unit is 100 ms
Keying5 ms raised-cosine rise and fall: no clicks
LevelPeak at −6 dBFS; the panel's own volume applies
File48 kHz, 16 bit, stereo WAV in the kit

One dot, enlarged: the soft start and end are the keying.

  • Only at start-up. Notifications, timers and the voice assistant have sounds of their own; the tone stays special.
  • Never faster, slower, higher or with effects: a tone that is not Morse is noise.
  • Where sound is not wanted (night, muted), the animation runs without it.

Medium (500) for the wordmark, headings and numbers that matter. Its round shapes with the odd sharp turn sit well next to dots and dashes. Tracking normal; headings slightly tighter (−0.01 em).

Type and voice

The name

One word, capital P, capital O and S: PanelOS. Said “panel O S”.

PanelOSPanel OSPanelOsPanelosPANELOSpanelOS
  • In running text the name stands alone: “PanelOS starts in twelve seconds”, not “the PanelOS”. With a noun it is fine: the PanelOS integration, a PanelOS panel.
  • In code, file names, the Home Assistant domain and URLs: panelos, all lower case.
  • The devices keep their own names: “an NSPanel Pro running PanelOS”. PanelOS is not a panel.
Short forms

The mark stands for the P. The signature spells P O S. There is no other abbreviation, and no nickname in public texts.

Next to other names

Home Assistant, ESPHome and other projects are named as they write themselves, and their logos keep their own rules. PanelOS never appears inside another logo, and no other logo is combined with the mark.

On the panel

  • Start-up: the stacked logo on Night with the quiet pattern, written in by the start-up sequence. The same picture is the app's first frame, so nothing jumps.
  • Screensaver and dashboard: no logo. The panel shows the home, not itself.
  • Setup and pairing carry the PanelOS logo, small, at the top; no other maker's logo appears on these screens. The accent is Signal, the grounds Night and Graphite, the text Paper: the palette of these guidelines throughout.
  • Settings: wherever the panel offers its settings, the button is the PanelOS mark, not a gear.

Why it looks like this

A mark for open firmware should be honest about what it is. This one is a letter, written in a code that was made to be read by anyone.

Pictures of screens, houses, waves and rings stand for many smart home products. These four elements of Morse code belong to PanelOS: they spell its initial, they read the same either way up, and they look like the screen it draws, with small and wide cards.

Red marks only what is lit, as on a panel at night. Everything else is ink and paper, so the identity is calm in a home, clear at 16 pixels, and cheap to print in one colour.

The same code makes the pattern, the start-up and its tone. One idea, used everywhere, is easier to remember than many.
